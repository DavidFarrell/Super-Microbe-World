Splash, cutscene and shrinking zone

The opening of the game, from the TV on the splash to the moment the chosen child is shrunk. The Flash references are Ruffle captures of the 2009 build and, where it helps, the same screen composed from the original SWFs without its text and logos.

Splash: the TV tuning in

Compare: The wooden TV, the room, the static and the green "Tuning" bars. Both run the TV animation to the same 170-frame timeline (6.8 s).

Deliberate differences:
  • Enter, Space or a tap skips the tuning to the menu; in Flash the whole animation always played (NOTES 2.1).

Splash: New Game

Compare: The final frame: the studio on the TV, the glossy blue New Game button in the same place, the dial and speaker grille.

Deliberate differences:
  • The TV shows a "Super Microbe World" title instead of the e-Bug logo, and both e-Bug smileys (podium and glass sticker) are erased; the podium carries a "?" medallion (NOTES 11.2; art log section 6).
  • Level select, Settings and a language badge are added under the TV; Continue appears once a journey is saved (NOTES 11.1, decision 3; NOTES 2.8 item 7).
  • No red FPS counter (a debug leftover in the original, NOTES 10.2 #49).

The platform levels

Each level shows its opening view and a moment of play. Things that differ on every level, and are not repeated below: the red FPS counter is gone (NOTES 10.2 #49); the clock shows 180 from the start instead of "90" and ends at 0 (NOTES 10.1 #8); the HUD's unused stopwatch is shown next to it and the clock turns red in the last 20 s; the "e-Bug" wordmark is removed from the ePhone (NOTES 3.24; NOTES 11.2). The Flash moments come from holding Right for 1.5 s and tapping Up; the remake moments are taken part way through each level's recorded winning input trace (web/tests/traces), so the positions differ. The desktop keyboard layout is shown; the touch layout is further down.

The ePhone briefing

Compare: The ePhone grown to landscape over the undimmed level and HUD, the host's head, the page text in white, the microbe picture and name.

Deliberate differences:
  • Spelling slips are corrected and the key names follow the device in use ("Press C", "tap the camera button"); the wording is otherwise the original's (NOTES 7.4).
  • The "e-Bug" wordmark on the side of the phone is removed (NOTES 11.2). Rapid clicks can no longer skip pages (NOTES 10.1 #17).

Summary card: out of time, or out of lives

Compare: The white panel with a black border over the frozen level, dimmed by a 50% black layer so the orange turns brown; the heading and the prompt to try again.

Deliberate differences:
  • A retry restarts the same level with the score kept, where Flash went back to the round's first level with its briefing; Settings can restore the original rule (NOTES 11.9, question 5; NOTES 10.2 #48).
  • The "out of lives" card is opened here with the parameters the flow passes after a death, over a level part way through; the "out of time" card is real (Amy left alone on level 1 for 180 s, as in capture 509).

Controls, pause and settings

The original was played with the mouse and keyboard only and had no pause menu or settings. The remake is designed for touch and keyboard equally, with gamepad support.

Keyboard layout on a desktop, touch layout on a phone

Compare: The keyboard layout keeps the original HUD exactly: score top right, hearts below it, clock top centre, ePhone bottom right.

Deliberate differences:
  • On touch screens the ePhone moves to the left edge and the pause and phone buttons sit top left, because thumbs and the action buttons cover the bottom right; controls fade when they pass over the player or a target (level1-compare.md; NOTES 3.24).
  • The on-screen controls hide after a key press and come back on the next touch; keys can be remapped in Settings (NOTES 11.1, decision 10).

Flash original (2009)

Remake

Pause menu New in the remake

Original: No Flash counterpart.

Remake:
  • Esc, the touch pause button, a hidden tab, a lost window focus or turning a phone to portrait pause the game. The card offers Resume, Restart, Settings, Level select and Quit, every button at least 44 CSS px on a phone (NOTES 11.1, decisions 9 and 10).

The game show

The quiz round after each action. The remake plays the live 2009 flow with blind rounds off by default: each round's questions are asked once, scored, after the action (NOTES 11.9, question 1; NOTES 2.3).

Ending and level select

The original ended on one host line that asked the player to reload the page, and its click did nothing. The remake adds a winner card and a level select, as the 2011 evaluation recommended.

The ending

Compare: The studio with both final scores on the podia and the host's closing line in the talkie.

Deliberate differences:
  • The win line names the real opponent and no longer says "reload this web page"; a tie is a draw with its own line (NOTES 10.2 #64; NOTES 11.9, question 3).
  • A winner card follows with the quiz points (which decide the winner, as in Flash), the hoverboard and kitchen points, and Play again, Level select and Main menu (NOTES 10.2 #46; NOTES 11.1, decision 6).

About these pictures

  • Ruffle captures were made by tools/ruffle/journey.cjs (see reference/captures/index.md and reference/analysis/flash-ruffle.md). Ruffle substitutes Noto Sans for the Arial and Verdana the original took from the player's computer, so treat their typography as layout only (NOTES 8.6). The e-Bug logos visible in them are the original's; the remake removes them (NOTES 11.2).
  • Composed screens come from tools/swf-sheet/compose.mjs: every element is a Ruffle render of the original symbol and every position comes from the SWFs, with dynamic text, logos and animation phase left out (screenshots/reference/index.md).
  • Remake screenshots are taken by tools/build-gallery.mjs from the game in web/, driven through real key presses and touch taps with the engine stepped tick by tick (?manual=1), so they are the same on every run. Regenerate with node tools/build-gallery.mjs (--only <id> for one session, --html-only for this page).
  • The older level 1 study, with the touch and keyboard layouts side by side, is in level1-compare.md.
  • Not shown: the preloader (the remake loads per area with a progress bar instead), the language chooser on first run, and the Flash-era prototypes in the capture set (level editor, older platformer and fridge game builds), which the remake does not recreate.
