**Title:** The e-Bug Platform Game
**Web:** http://www.e-bug.eu

## Project Introduction

- The e-Bug project is a European-wide, EC funded, antibiotic and hygiene teaching resource.
- e-Bug reinforces an awareness of microbes, hand and respiratory hygiene and the benefits of prudent antibiotic use.
- e-Bug consists of two lesson packs which will be made available to junior and senior schools and a website with educational games.

## Objectives

- to create a game targeted at and suitable for 9-11 year olds
- to create a game that is enjoyable enough to be played by the audience in their own discretionary time
- to create a game which compliments but can be used independantly of the e-Bug pack
- to cover a series of agreed learning outcomes as defined by the project's domain experts

## Methods

- regular discussions and meetings with the project's domain experts to check scientific validity of game designs and implementation
- focus groups and email based iteration of game designs with teachers
- examination of popular games and media targeting our audience
- regular playtests with pupils

## Game Flow

**Game Show Intro and Questions.** The Game Show is used to ask the questions that the player will explore in the next action section. The answers are not given. After answering, the player starts either a number of platform game levels, or plays the sorting game.

**Hoverboard Game Round.** Each round focuses on different learning outcomes and uses different types of missions and different environments to cover these.

**Sorting Game.** The food sorting round covers topics that were difficult to deal with at the 'micro' scale - such as food hygiene, sneezing and handwashing.

**Game Show Summary Questions.** At the end of each action section, the player's learning is tested by asking multiple choice questions. These can be compared to answers given earlier to test for improvement. After answering, the game show advances and the player is given another introduction and set of questions.

## Game Show Theme

- each section of the game is connected by a game show that the player takes part in
- *(callout)* scores provide an incentive to answer questions correctly and encourage repeat play
- *(callout)* questions are asked before and after each section to encourage critical thinking and assess learning

## Platform Game Sections

- the player is "shrunk" to allow interaction with microbes
- different missions such as "photograph bacteria" are used to convey learning outcomes
- environments (screenshot labels): in the kitchen / on skin / inside the body

## Sorting Game Section (image callouts)

- player has a small amount of time to correctly store food
- player must use tissue when sneezing or will ruin the current food item
- raw meat must be wrapped with film
- items put in the fridge must be placed on correct shelf (no raw meat on high, barred shelves etc.)
- player must wash hands after handling raw meat or sneezing
- spoiled items must be put in the bin

## Learning Outcomes (with the game mechanic callouts that sit next to them)

### Introduction to Microbes

- bacteria, viruses and fungi are three different types of microbe
- microbes are found everywhere
- microbes come in different shapes and sizes
- *(callout)* fungi are shown larger than bacteria which are larger than viruses

### Good and Bad Microbes

- good microbes can keep us healthy
- bacteria can be put to good use
- sometimes microbes can make us ill
- *(callout)* good microbes prevent bad microbes from entering the body
- *(callout)* player throws "natural body defenses" to kill bad microbes

### Hand, Respiratory and Food Hygiene

- infection can be spread through unclean hands
- hand washing can prevent the spread of infection
- infection can be spread through sneezing and coughing
- sneezing or coughing in your hand can still spread infection
- microbes can be found on our food and can transfer to humans
- cooking food properly can kill harmful microbes
- *(callout)* player washes bad microbes off skin

### Antibiotic and Vaccine Use

- most common infections get better on their own through time, rest, liquid intake and healthy living
- if antibiotics are taken, it is important to finish the course
- there are not vaccines for all infections
- *(callout)* player delivers antibiotics to heal a "serious infection"

## Future Work

- Work on tools to support game mechanic based teaching
- Cognitive Walkthrough for Learning Through Game Mechanics
- Have self and other designers investigate usefulness of the CWLTGM.

The game is currently live and being used across Europe. In paralell to this, we are developing a sequel in Unity to allow redesign of less successful areas by other designers to test CWLTGM
